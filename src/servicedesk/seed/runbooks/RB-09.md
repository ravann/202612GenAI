# RB-09 Incident priority matrix and escalation

## Priorities

| Priority | Meaning | First response | Target to restore |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | A customer-facing service is down or unusable, or data is at risk | 15 minutes | 4 hours |
| P2 | A service is badly degraded, or more than 25 users cannot work | 30 minutes | 8 hours |
| P3 | Some users are affected and a workaround exists | 4 hours | 3 working days |
| P4 | A minor issue or a question | 1 working day | 10 working days |

## How to choose

1. Start from the impact: how many users, and is the service customer-facing?
2. Raise the priority by one level if the problem blocks payroll, month-end close or a legal deadline.
3. When the requester and the service desk disagree, the service desk decides and writes the reason in the incident.

## Escalation

- P1: page the service owner and inform a change manager straight away. Post an update every 30 minutes.
- P2: inform the service owner within 30 minutes. Post an update every 2 hours.
- P3 and P4: no escalation unless the target is at risk.

## Closing an incident

An incident is `resolved` when the service works again, and `closed` after the requester confirms or after 3 working days without a reply.
