# RB-06 Orders database failover (db-04 to db-05)

`db-04` (dc-east, storage on `san-01`) is the primary orders database. `db-05` (dc-west, storage on `san-02`) is its standby replica. Checkout (`app-checkout-01`) and the CRM (`app-crm-01`) both connect to the primary through the alias `orders-db`.

## When to use this runbook

- `db-04` is down, or
- `db-04` is so slow that checkout misses its latency target for more than 30 minutes and the cause is on the database host or its storage.

A failover is a production change. Raise a change request first (RB-05). In a P1 incident, follow the emergency change rule in RB-05.

## Before you start

1. Check replication lag on `db-05`. It must be under 30 seconds. If it is higher, wait or stop non-essential writes first; failing over with lag loses recent orders.
2. Tell the checkout and CRM teams. Both services pause for about 2 minutes.
3. Note the time; you need it for the incident record.

## Steps

1. Put checkout into maintenance mode on `lb-01`.
2. Stop writes on `db-04` with `dbctl readonly db-04`.
3. Wait until `db-05` has applied all changes (lag 0).
4. Promote the standby with `dbctl promote db-05`.
5. Point the alias `orders-db` at `db-05`.
6. Take checkout out of maintenance mode and place one test order.
7. Confirm that CRM customer search works.

## Rollback

If `db-05` misbehaves within the first 30 minutes, point `orders-db` back at `db-04` and make it writable again. After 30 minutes, rolling back needs a fresh copy from `db-05` (about 2 hours); treat that as a new change.

## Afterwards

Rebuild `db-04` as the new standby once its problem is fixed, and record the failover in the incident and the change request.
